Photo Drop · dev.photodrop.app · UI/UX overhaul

Three directions for a control panel that only has one job to do.

Photo Drop starts a server that lives in Termux and hands you a link. That is the entire product: start it, send it, know there's room. Everything below is judged against that — a five-second, one-handed interaction, usually in a hurry, on a phone held in daylight. The current app is a settings page where that answer is scrolled off the top and the two buttons in the middle are permanently half-dead.

ALive Header

The status strip owns the top of the screen and changes colour for every state, so "is it on?" is answered before you read a word. One primary button that changes identity — Start when dead, Copy when live — replaces the Start/Stop pair where one is always disabled. Everything below the strip is one scroll of progressively less important detail. Most conservative, most legible in sun, smallest diff from the current code.

CTap to Toggle

Make the server itself the interface. A single large orb is the switch — tap to start, tap to stop — and the whole screen goes green when it is live, so state is felt before it is read. The link and its URL slide up from the bottom sheet only once there is something to show. The most animated of the three and the most work: it needs a real sheet transition and a hold-to-stop affordance so an accidental tap does not kill an upload in progress.

What the overhaul has to fix

1 · Nothing has presence
Every useful fact — is it on, where is the link, is there room — is below the fold. The 7-second poll changes text with no transition, so the app feels dead even when it is working.
2 · Two buttons, one alive
Start is greyed while running; Stop is at 45% alpha while stopped. Half the primary surface is always inert. All three directions collapse this to one control that changes identity.
3 · The good news is missing
tvUploads is hardcoded to "No uploads in this session yet" forever. The server already returns sessions in /healthz. "Your friend's photos are landing" is the most reassuring thing this app could say.
4 · The hero fights itself
Overline, domain, URL, hint, 48dp button, text toggle and a collapsible QR box in one box. All three directions cut the toggle — the QR is either permanent or the screen is the QR.
5 · Toast-only feedback
Every action is a Toast: unstacked, unstateful, and gone in two seconds. Copy confirmation belongs on the button, and errors need a persistent, explained recovery.
6 · Inert where it matters
When stopped — which is every first open — the main surface is a greyed box reading "Server not started". No onboarding, no explanation of the two one-time chores.
